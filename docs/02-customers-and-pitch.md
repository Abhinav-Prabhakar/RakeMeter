# 02 · The paying customers: how they work today, how to pitch, and the proof

*Rules and rates below are taken from the Railway Board's "Compendium: Demurrage, Stabling, Wharfage, Stacking, Waiver and Write off (as on 03.04.2024)" unless marked otherwise. Day-to-day practice inside plants is drawn from job listings, vendor material and published studies, and is marked **(to verify)** where it needs confirming in interviews.*

## 1. Who they are

| Segment | What moves | Typical siding | Why they feel the pain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermal power plants (private and state) | Coal in, in BOXN open wagons or BOBRN hoppers | 2–10+ rakes a day, wagon tipplers or track hoppers | Highest rake counts; bunched arrivals; coal is ~51% of all rail tonnage |
| Steel plants | Coal, ore, limestone in; finished steel out | Multiple lines, tipplers, loading bays | Both directions; congestion can trigger penal rates up to 6× |
| Cement plants | Coal, clinker, gypsum in; bagged/bulk cement out | 1–3 rakes a day | Logistics is 30–35% of delivered cost (JSW Cement's figure) |
| Mines and washeries | Coal or ore out | Loaders, silos, in-motion weighbridge | Overloading and underloading both bite on every rake |
| Private freight / Gati Shakti terminals, ports | Mixed, for many customers | Multi-user terminals | They resell handling; delays hit their margin and their customers |
| Container train operators | Containers | Inland depots | Small segment, few buyers; park for later |

**Start with:** mid-size plants and terminal operators taking 1–6 rakes a day. The largest groups (Tata Steel, JSW, Jindal) are listed as clients of existing vendors and often build in-house.

## 2. How a rake is handled today

### Inbound (unloading), step by step

1. **Dispatch.** The supplier loads at origin. The plant gets an FNR, the 11-digit number that identifies the consignment in FOIS.
2. **Watching it come.** Someone looks the FNR up on the railway's FOIS / Freight Business Development portal, or phones the divisional control office. **(to verify)** ETAs are passed around by phone and messaging groups.
3. **Arrival and placement.** The rake reaches the serving station, then is placed in the siding by a railway or plant locomotive. **Free time starts at placement.** The railway's goods clerk records the time.
4. **Unloading.** Open wagons go through a wagon tippler one by one; hopper wagons discharge over a track hopper. Shunting, tippler allocation and crew are coordinated by radio and paper registers (Adani Power's own job listing describes this coordination as manual).
5. **Release.** Empties are checked and formed up, and the plant declares the rake released. **Free time stops at release of the last wagon.**
6. **Billing.** If release was late, the railway raises a demurrage bill. Accounts pays it.
7. **Disputes.** If the plant thinks the delay wasn't its fault, it applies for a waiver.

### Outbound (loading)

1. **Indent.** The plant places a demand on FOIS (e-Demand) and pays a wagon registration fee of ₹50,000 per rake (₹2 lakh for iron ore).
2. **Empty rake placed.** Free time starts.
3. **Loading.** By pay-loader, conveyor or silo. Operators aim for each wagon's permissible carrying capacity by eye, by bucket count, or with a pre-weigh hopper.
4. **Weighment.** The loaded rake crosses a nominated in-motion weighbridge. Weighment of all rakes is compulsory with few exceptions.
5. **If overloaded.** Punitive charges are raised. Gross overloads must be adjusted (material removed), and the whole rake accrues demurrage while that happens.
6. **Railway receipt** is issued on the weighed weight, and the rake departs.

## 3. The rules that create the cost

### Free time (non-steel-plant sidings)

| Wagon type | Mechanised loading | Mechanised unloading | Manual loading | Manual unloading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open (BOXN etc.) | 5 h | 7 h | 9 h | 9 h |
| Hopper (BOBR etc.) | 5 h | 2 h 30 | — | — |
| Flat (BRN, BFK etc.) | 6 h | — | 8 h | 8 h |
| Covered (BCN), 31+ wagons | — | — | 9 h | 9 h |
| Tank | 6 h | 6–8 h | — | — |
| Container | 3 h | 3 h | — | — |

- At engine-on-load terminals the windows are tighter: open rake 3 h loading (6 h for steel) and 5 h unloading; hopper rake 3 h and 2 h.
- Steel plants have their own table: 14 h each way for the seven old plants; 12 h loading and 8 h unloading for others.
- A 2024 circular notes zonal railways cut mechanised unloading of coal and iron-ore rakes from 7 h to 5 h in places.
- Private sidings not charged on through-distance get 2 extra hours for shunting.
- **Bunching allowance:** 3 extra hours when rakes arrive bunched beyond the siding's handling capacity. It has to be claimed and evidenced.
- One extra hour per rake for tarpaulin covering.

### Demurrage rate

- **₹150 per 8-wheeled wagon per hour, or part of an hour.**
- **The whole rake is one unit.** If one wagon is late, every wagon is charged.
- Graded: +10% beyond 6 h over, +25% beyond 12 h, +50% beyond 24 h, 2× beyond 48 h, 3× beyond 72 h.
- **Penal demurrage:** during congestion the railway can raise the rate up to 6× with 24 hours' notice.

### Waiver

- First application within **10 days** of the charge accruing (large sidings such as power houses and steel plants: by the end of the next month).
- The charge must be **paid in full first**, with proof attached.
- Powers are per wagon: ₹25,000 at divisional manager level, up to full powers at additional general manager level.
- Grounds are grouped as within the customer's control, beyond it (strikes, bandhs, etc.), or the railway's own doing.
- Waivers are common: a CAG review found ₹267 crore waived in the cases it audited.

### Overloading and underloading

- Punitive charges apply above permissible carrying capacity plus a tolerance of 1 or 2 tonnes depending on wagon type, as a multiple of freight that rises with the excess and rises again if the rake as a whole is over. The schedule was last amended in 2022. **The current multipliers need to be read from the gazette before we quote them**; the superseded 2007 schedule ran from 2× to 5× freight.
- The railway's stated stance is zero tolerance, but its own officials acknowledge many loading points lack a working weighbridge, so overloads are found later.
- Underloading costs the other way: a CAG audit found one coal company paid ₹38.64 crore in underloading charges over three years, with underloading rising from about 52 to 321 tonnes per rake where weighbridges were missing.

## 4. The proof: what an hour is worth

All figures follow directly from the rate rules above for a 58-wagon BOXN rake.

| Hours past free time | Rate applied | Charge for that rake |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | base | ₹8,700 |
| 3 | base | ₹26,100 |
| 6 | base | ₹52,200 |
| 10 | 6 h base + 4 h at +10% | ₹90,480 |
| 24 | graded | ₹2,40,120 |

*The 10 h and 24 h rows assume each higher rate applies only to the hours inside its own band. If the railway applies the higher rate to the whole overstay, those two figures are larger. Confirm against a real bill.*

**"Part of an hour" matters.** A rake released 5 minutes into a new hour pays the full ₹8,700. Shaving 20 minutes can remove an entire hour's charge.

### Annual exposure by plant size

Charge = rakes/day × hours over × ₹8,700 × 365.

| Rakes a day | 1 h average overstay | 2 h | 3 h |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ₹32 lakh | ₹64 lakh | ₹95 lakh |
| 3 | ₹95 lakh | ₹1.9 crore | ₹2.9 crore |
| 6 | ₹1.9 crore | ₹3.8 crore | ₹5.7 crore |

Outside evidence that overstays of this size are normal:

- A published study of an Indian thermal power plant found bunched ("successive") arrivals alone accounted for about **8.5 hours of demurrage per rake on average**, with the tippler outlet as the bottleneck. *(Read from the abstract only; get the full paper before quoting.)*
- A competitor estimates ₹15–23 lakh a month lost at a plant taking three rakes a day and overshooting by 2–3 hours **(vendor claim)**. That is consistent with the table above.
- CAG audits repeatedly record crores in demurrage at individual sidings (for example ₹8.95 crore across six sidings in one review).

### Where software removes hours, without new equipment

| Lever | Mechanism | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|
| Know the rake is coming | Crew, loco and tippler ready at placement rather than found afterwards | Vendor claim of 2–6 h planning lead; to measure in pilot |
| Sequence bunched rakes | Decide which rake goes to which line first to minimise total charge, since each rake's clock runs separately | Arithmetic; the power-plant study |
| Beat the hour boundary | Live clock showing the next ₹8,700 step | Arithmetic |
| Claim what is already owed | Bunching allowance, shunting time, tarpaulin hour, placement gaps over 2 h | Rulebook; depends on records |
| Dispute wrong bills | Check the railway's bill against our own timestamps | Rulebook; CAG waiver figures |
| File waivers on time | 10-day window, evidence pack ready | Rulebook |
| Load to the tolerance | Per-wagon targets so neither punitive charges nor idle freight | CAG underloading audit |

A competitor claims a 10–25% cut in turnaround **(vendor claim)**. Using their own example: with 5 h free time and an 8 h turnaround, a 15% cut removes 1.2 h, which is 40% of the chargeable 3 hours.

### Two more worked examples

**Underloading.** If each of 58 wagons leaves 2 tonnes under capacity, the rake carries 116 tonnes less than it paid freight for. At a freight rate of ₹F per tonne that is 116 × F per rake. At two rakes a day and, say, ₹1,000 a tonne, about ₹8.5 crore a year. *(₹1,000 is a placeholder; use the customer's actual rate.)*

**A single avoidable hour.** One rake a day released in hour 8 rather than hour 7 costs ₹8,700 × 365 = ₹31.8 lakh a year. That alone would cover a typical software subscription.

## 5. How to pitch it

### Who to talk to

| Person | What they care about | What to show them |
|---|---|---|
| Head of rail logistics / siding in-charge | Turnaround, not being blamed for bills | Live rake clock and the shift plan |
| Plant or works head | Cost per tonne, throughput | Annual rupees at risk and recovered |
| Finance / commercial | Bills, provisions, audit trail | Bill audit and waiver tracker |
| IT | Integration effort, security | No PLC work, ERP export, data stays theirs |

### The opening offer: a free charge audit

Ask for six months of three things they already have: demurrage and punitive bills, placement/release times, and weighment slips. Return within a week:

1. Total charged, by month, rake and shift.
2. How much was **avoidable** (overstay under one hour past a boundary, bunched arrivals where allowance wasn't claimed, bills that don't match the records).
3. How much could have been **recovered** (waivers not filed or filed late).
4. Over- and underloading per wagon, in rupees.

This makes their own numbers do the pitching, costs them nothing, and tells us whether the account is worth pursuing.

### The pitch in four sentences

> You paid the railway ₹X in demurrage and ₹Y in overloading charges last year. Our audit of your own records shows ₹Z of that was avoidable with no new equipment. We show every rake's clock in rupees, plan the yard so the expensive rakes go first, and check every bill against what actually happened. We charge a fixed fee per siding, and the savings ledger shows each month whether we earned it.

### Pilot terms

- One siding, 8–12 weeks, software only.
- Baseline from the audit; success measured against it on three numbers: average hours past free time, demurrage per rake, and rupees recovered through disputes.
- Priced low or as a share of measured savings, converting to an annual subscription.

### Objections to expect

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Delays are the railway's fault." | Then the product's job is to prove it, with timestamps, and get the charge waived. |
| "We already track rakes on FOIS." | Tracking tells you where the rake is. It doesn't tell you what the next hour costs or which rake to unload first. |
| "We have SAP." | SAP records the bill after it's paid. We sit before the bill. |
| "Our vendor is automating the tippler." | Good; that speeds up unloading. We cover the clock, the yard plan, loading and the bills, and work alongside it. |
| "Data security." | Runs on their records; no control of plant equipment; export to ERP. |

## 6. What to confirm in the first interviews

1. Who records placement and release times, and how often do the plant's and the railway's records differ?
2. Annual demurrage and punitive charges paid, and what share is waived.
3. How they learn a rake is coming, and how many hours ahead.
4. How loading targets per wagon are set, and typical over/under per rake.
5. Whether they can export FOIS data, and what their agreement with the railway allows.
6. Who would sign for a pilot, and what budget line it comes from.
